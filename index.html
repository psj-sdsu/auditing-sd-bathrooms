About the project

Why document restroom access?

Public restroom access is an important part of water, sanitation, hygiene, mobility, and public-space access.

This tool supports field-based documentation of public restroom conditions across San Diego County. Researchers and volunteers can review an existing location, suggest a new restroom, and record observations about access, accessibility, amenities, signage, safety, and surrounding conditions.

The public map is separated from the review process: a submission does not automatically become public. Project staff review proposed additions and changes before they are incorporated into the working restroom dataset.

Field workflow

How an audit works

01

Choose a location

Select an existing restroom on the map or choose a map location to suggest a new restroom.

02

Complete the assessment

Record the audit date, access conditions, hours, accessibility, amenities, safety indicators, and field observations.

03

Submit for review

Your audit is saved for project-team review. Unapproved submissions do not immediately change the public map.

04

Publish approved changes

Approved additions and updates are incorporated into the working restroom dataset used by the map.

Before you begin

Plan for a careful field assessment.

✓

Allow enough time to evaluate the facility. An assessment may take approximately 30 minutes or more.

✓

When possible, evaluate both the exterior and interior of the restroom facility.

✓

Only enter publicly accessible areas and follow posted facility rules and restrictions.
